# Report formats

## Choosing a format

| Situation | Format | Typical length |
|---|---|---|
| A quick answer or a time-sensitive question | Quick brief | A few short paragraphs |
| Most research requests | Research summary (default) | One to two pages |
| Choosing between options | Comparison | One to two pages plus a table |
| Formal, strategic or high-stakes work | Comprehensive report | As long as the evidence needs |

## Quick brief

1. Answer in two or three sentences.
2. Key points, each with a citation.
3. What we do not know yet.
4. Sources.

## Research summary

1. Summary: the most important finding and its implication.
2. Findings by theme, each with evidence and citations.
3. Conflicts and stale information, with dates.
4. Recommendations or next steps, if asked for.
5. Open questions.
6. Sources.

## Comparison

1. The decision and the criteria, with weights if the user has them.
2. Comparison table: options as columns, criteria as rows, each cell cited or marked unknown.
3. Discussion of each option's strengths, weaknesses and risks.
4. Recommendation with rationale, if asked for, and what would change it.
5. Sources.

## Comprehensive report

1. Executive summary: a standalone paragraph.
2. Background and scope: the question, what was searched and what was out of scope.
3. Method: queries, filters, number of pages reviewed, selection criteria.
4. Findings by theme, with evidence.
5. Analysis: patterns, conflicts, implications.
6. Recommendations, prioritized, with rationale.
7. Risks and limitations, including gaps in the workspace record.
8. Appendix: data tables.
9. Sources, grouped as primary, supporting and background.

## Citation rules

- Cite right after the claim with a page mention, for example `<mention-page url="URL-from-fetch">Page title</mention-page>`, or a plain link if the target does not accept mentions.
- Group several claims from one source under one citation rather than repeating it on every sentence.
- Mark direct quotes as quotes and attribute them.
- When citing database content, mention the database or the specific entries.
- Note when a source is old: "last edited March 2024; may be superseded".
- For results from connected sources (chat, drive, issue trackers), say which system they came from; readers may lack access to that system.

## Source-quality rubric

| Signal | Stronger | Weaker |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Named owner, verified page | No owner, personal notes |
| Location | Team hub, official database | Scratch pages, archive |
| Recency | Edited within its review cadence | Not edited for a long time |
| Specificity | Concrete numbers, dates, decisions | Vague or aspirational |
| Corroboration | Agrees with other sources | Contradicted or isolated |

## Handling conflicts

State both claims with their sources and dates, say which is likely current and why (newer, owned, verified, referenced by later work), and list the conflict as an open question for the owner. Never silently pick one.
